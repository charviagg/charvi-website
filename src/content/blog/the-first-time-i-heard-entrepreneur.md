description: "At eleven, a quilling exhibition became my unexpected introduction to entrepreneurship."

I was eleven when I first heard the word *entrepreneur*.

It started with quilling — the art of turning thin strips of paper into something entirely new. I spent time creating handmade envelopes, photo frames, clocks, and other pieces, eventually putting together an exhibition of my work.

Everything sold out on the first day.

The experience led to a feature in *The Times of India*, where I was described as the youngest entrepreneur. Until then, I hadn't really known what the word meant.

Looking back, what stays with me isn't just the exhibition or the newspaper feature. It's the excitement of making something from scratch and seeing other people connect with it.

That curiosity has stayed with me.

Over the years, I've found myself exploring very different fields — from consulting projects with SMEs in India to working on a data-centre project in Singapore. I've enjoyed being challenged to learn something unfamiliar, ask questions, and discover how things work.

I've always wanted to be a creator whose work has an impact.

And I think that eleven-year-old version of me was already beginning to figure that out.
